# Design Context — MV Hofkirchen Inventar

Gathered 2026-09-04 via `/impeccable:teach-impeccable`. Source for all design-skill work (audit, normalize, polish, critique, …).

## Design Context

### Users

Three roles, three contexts, one small brass band (Musikverein Hofkirchen, Austria). UI language is German (formal-neutral, Austrian spelling: "Schließen", de-AT dates and currency).

- **Instrumenten-/Zeugwart at a desk.** One or two officers keeping the inventory: registering instruments, uniforms and general items, filing invoices, recording loans and returns. Laptop or desktop, unhurried, working through lists and forms. They need accurate data entry, fast search, and confidence that nothing was saved twice or lost.
- **Officers on a phone in the Probelokal.** The same people handing out or taking back instruments and uniforms during rehearsal: standing, one-handed, often poor light. They need to find a person or an item in three taps, see loan status at a glance, and record a return without a form.
- **Notenwart scanning sheet music.** Longer focused sessions at a desk with a scanner and a large screen, or on a tablet with fingers or a pen, reviewing symbol detections on the scan canvas, correcting them, and generating LilyPond output. Precision and legibility at any zoom matter more than speed.

Not users: the general membership. There is no login; access is controlled by Cloudflare Tunnel. The tool is internal.

### Brand Personality

**Calm, dependable, crafted.**

The interface should feel like a well-kept ledger: quiet, trustworthy, nothing flashy. The data is the hero. Emotional goals: confidence when saving, clarity when scanning a list, patience during long scanner analyses (clear progress, honest error messages, no dead ends).

The club's identity comes from its logo: a hand-engraved illustration of two ibex in blue uniform jackets with red trim, playing trumpet and horn under a green "MVH" banner. The interface should carry that heritage lightly: through colour and typographic care, not through illustration or ornament on every page.

### Aesthetic Direction

- **Palette from the uniform.** Primary is the jacket blue (a saturated, slightly muted uniform blue, not Tailwind navy and not sky-cyan). Neutrals are tinted toward that blue, never pure grey, never pure white or black. The green banner is reserved for success and "available" states. The red trim is the danger colour. Warm amber for warnings. Every colour has a light and a dark variant; dark mode keeps the same character, not an inversion.
- **Both themes are first-class.** Light and dark already exist and are chosen by system preference or toggle. Any new colour is defined as a token pair. White text on the primary must be re-checked in dark mode.
- **Typography carries the personality.** DM Sans is the working body face and stays. Hierarchy comes from size, weight and spacing on a fixed scale; uppercase labels get letter-spacing; digits in tables are tabular.
- **Surfaces are flat and quiet.** Borders and background tints separate regions; shadow is reserved for things that float (menus, dialogs). No card-inside-card, no accent bar on every card, no identical card grids. Tables and definition lists are preferred over tiles for data.
- **Motion is functional.** Transitions signal state changes only; transform and opacity, exponential ease-out, and everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **Anti-references.** Generic Tailwind admin dashboards; hero-metric tiles with big numbers; cyan-on-dark "developer tool" looks; glassmorphism; gradient anything; playful or "startup" styling. Also not a museum piece: the engraving is inspiration for colour, not a texture to reproduce.

### Design Principles

1. **Ledger first.** Lists, tables and detail pages are the product. Optimise for scanning a column, finding a row, and reading a value. Decoration that does not help that job is removed.
2. **Every action has a name and a keyboard.** Anything clickable is a `<button>` or a link with a visible label or `aria-label`. Dialogs use native `<dialog>`. Nothing depends on hover or mouse alone. Touch targets are at least 44 px in the scanner and on any control likely to be used on a phone.
3. **Tell the truth about state.** Loading, empty, error and "running in the background" are all distinct, visible, and worded in plain German. Errors appear inline where they happened, never as `alert()`. Destructive actions confirm; long operations show progress.
4. **One token, both themes.** Colours, radii, shadows and spacing come from `style.css` tokens only. A new colour is added as a light/dark pair with checked contrast, never as a literal in a component.
5. **Adapt, don't shrink.** The phone view of a list is a card list with the same actions; the tablet view of the scanner keeps the canvas usable with fingers; the desktop view shows navigation without a hamburger. Hiding a feature on a smaller screen is not an option.
